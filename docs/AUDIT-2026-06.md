# Full App Audit — June 2026

_Padeltrainer pre-launch consolidated audit. Generated 2026-06-12._

This report merges six independent investigation tracks into one prioritized
picture. Findings are de-duplicated across tracks and graded P0 (ship-blocker) →
P3 (polish). Every money/data finding carries a `file:line` evidence anchor so an
engineer can jump straight to the code. Scenarios are written in plain language —
you should be able to understand every P0 and P1 without reading a line of code.

IDs are namespaced by domain: **M** = Money/Invoicing, **U** = UX/Errors,
**E** = Edge functions, **D** = Data integrity, **P** = Performance,
**ENV** = Environment/secrets.

### The five most dangerous findings (plain language)

1. **Your draft invoices silently collapse to a fraction of the real price (M-01).**
   When a trainer books a split-payment cycle, or adds a drop-in player to an
   existing split slot, an automatic recalculation runs in the same click and
   divides the per-player amount *again*. A €241 invoice quietly becomes €120
   before the trainer ever opens it. The trainer then sends the customer a bill
   for half (or a seventh) of what they're owed, and never notices.

2. **A customer pays for a split cycle, the money lands in the trainer's Mollie
   account, but the booking is never confirmed (M-02).** A rounding mismatch in
   the payment check rejects perfectly valid payments for any multi-session cycle
   whose total isn't an exact multiple of the session count — i.e. most of them.
   The player's bank shows the charge; your system shows nothing. No invoice, no
   confirmation email, no automatic retry. Recovery is manual, one case at a time.

3. **Anyone on the internet can email-bomb any address through your own
   endpoints (E-01, E-02).** Two edge functions — the auth magic-link sender and
   the onboarding-email processor — accept an arbitrary recipient with no login
   and no rate limit. A loop of `curl` calls floods a victim's inbox with your
   "reset your password" and onboarding mails, and burns your Resend sending
   reputation. CORS is wide open, so this can even be driven from a victim's own
   browser.

4. **Cancelled invoices can come back from the dead as "paid", and you collect
   twice (M-12).** Cancelling an invoice never cancels the open Mollie checkout
   behind it. If a customer later completes that old checkout, the webhook flips
   the cancelled invoice to "paid" with no guard — so you've now collected on both
   the cancelled invoice and the corrected one you re-issued. Same shape for the
   common "edit a sent invoice while a pay link is live" flow (M-11).

5. **Bookings get double-billed because the duplicate-invoice guard has a hole
   (M-06, M-08).** The guard only catches an *exact* repeat of the same booking
   set. As cycle-commitment batches grow day-by-day, or when proposal-finalization
   overlaps with priority claims, the same session lands on two invoices and the
   player is charged twice — on invoices that auto-send with no human review.
   Compounding this: academy invoice numbers have **zero** uniqueness protection
   (M-10), so two admins can mint the same legal invoice number — a Dutch
   bookkeeping (Belastingdienst) violation.

## How this audit was run

Six tracks ran across roughly forty specialized agents. **T0** mapped the
environment and secrets posture. **T1** traced every money path (slot → booking →
invoice → payment) as actually implemented, building five state machines and
adversarially verifying each high-severity finding — every P0/P1 below survived a
dedicated reviewer who tried and failed to refute it, with arithmetic executed in
Node and concurrency races reproduced against the real constraints in PGlite.
**T2** ran a production data X-ray (counts only, no row contents) against two
notice-only migrations to check 23 integrity invariants on live data. **T3**
audited failure-mode UX — what a non-technical user actually sees when something
breaks. **T5** probed the edge-function surface (input validation, error leakage,
retry/double-delivery, public abuse). **T6** did static and dynamic performance
analysis with bundle inspection and query-plan simulation. **T4** — a live
click-through of the whole product as a non-technical Dutch user — is **pending
test-account credentials** and will be appended to this document when those land;
treat the UX findings here as static-analysis-derived until then.

## P0 findings

### M-01 — Automatic draft-invoice recalc re-divides already-per-player amounts; totals collapse to a fraction
- **What happens:** Split-payment bookings store each player's *share* (e.g. €25
  of a €50 slot). The auto-create-invoice function correctly writes a draft using
  that share and tags the line "(1/N)". Then, in the *same* add-player click, an
  automatic recalculation reloads that fresh draft, fails a strict equality check,
  decides the amounts "aren't split shares", and divides each amount by N **again**.
  The €25 share becomes €12.50 (or €3.13). The trainer never sees the correct
  number — the draft is rewritten milliseconds after it's created.
  - _Trigger A:_ booking a whole split cycle at an indivisible price (e.g. €49.98
    split 4 ways) — drift of exactly 0.02 fails the strict `< 0.02` guard.
  - _Trigger B (most common):_ adding a drop-in player to one slot of an existing
    split cycle. The other players' rows get rebalanced to a different amount, the
    recalc sees mixed amounts, and halves **every** session. A €241.67 invoice
    becomes €120.84.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/functions/auto-create-invoice/index.ts:122-138,299-303`
  (writes correct draft + "(1/N)" label); `src/lib/invoiceSync.ts:77-91`
  (`paymentAmountsAreSplitShares` strict guard), `:112-113/132-133/155-157`
  (re-divides); reached automatically via
  `src/lib/invoiceAfterAddPlayer.ts:263-270` →
  `src/lib/applyAffectedInvoiceUpdates.ts:37-48` →
  `syncInvoicesAfterPriceChange(statuses=['draft'])`. Verified by executing the
  exact expressions: 100/7 → unit €2.04; 49.98/4 → unit €3.13.
- **Blast radius (verifier-narrowed):** Trigger A hits non-divisible price/N
  combos (mostly N≥4); Trigger B hits **any** split cycle including the common
  N=2, after a single drop-in rebalance; rotating-membership cycles too. The same
  recalc path is reachable from slot-delete and price-edit flows, and corrupts
  *sent* invoices if the trainer confirms "update drafts and sent".
- **Fix size:** M

### M-02 — Per-booking rounding makes the payment guard reject legitimate paid cyclus payments; money taken, booking never confirmed
- **What happens:** When a player pays for a multi-session cycle, Mollie is
  charged the correct rounded total (e.g. €42.86), but each of the 10 bookings is
  stored with `round(total/10)` = €4.29. The webhook later re-sums those rounded
  rows (10 × 4.29 = €42.90), compares to the €42.86 actually paid, sees a €0.04
  gap, and **blocks** — returning HTTP 200 so Mollie never retries. The player's
  money is in the trainer's Mollie account; your platform has no record of the
  payment. Bookings stay pending forever; no invoice, no confirmation email. The
  success-page fallback (`verify-mollie-payment`) applies the identical check and
  also refuses.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/functions/create-mollie-payment/index.ts:461-465`
  (stores rounded per-booking amount), `:496` (charges exact total);
  `supabase/functions/mollie-webhook/index.ts:481-496` (sums and blocks);
  `verify-mollie-payment/index.ts:279-299` (same check). Proven numerically
  against the repo formulas.
- **Blast radius:** Every multi-session payment whose total isn't an exact
  multiple of the session count — i.e. all split-payment cycles with indivisible
  shares, plus any hourly-rate price with repeating decimals. 8–10 session cycles
  block for ~72–78% of possible charge amounts. **Verifier note:** the split
  divisor counts booking *rows* rather than distinct *players*, so the same code
  also massively **undercharges** split cycles (€4.29 instead of €42.86 for a
  7-player cycle) — a second critical defect riding alongside this one.
- **Fix size:** S

### E-01 — `send-auth-email`: unauthenticated, unthrottled magic-link / password-reset sender (email-bomb to any address)
- **What happens:** This function has `verify_jwt=false`, zero auth, and zero rate
  limiting. It accepts `{type, email}` from any caller and calls the Supabase
  **admin** `generateLink` API (which bypasses GoTrue's built-in reset throttle),
  then sends via Resend to whatever address was supplied. An attacker can POST
  `{type:"password_reset", email:<victim>}` in a loop to flood any inbox, or spray
  an address list. CORS is `*`, so it's drivable from any browser origin.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/config.toml:43` (`verify_jwt=false`);
  `supabase/functions/send-auth-email/index.ts:115-205,166,181,202`; invoked
  unauthenticated from the SPA at `src/lib/auth.ts:619`.
- **Blast radius:** Any email address on earth; your Resend sending reputation;
  victim trust ("why does Padeltrainer keep emailing me?").
- **Fix size:** S (add a service-role check or per-IP/per-recipient throttle)

### E-02 — `process-onboarding-emails`: unauthenticated test path sends a fully-rendered email to any caller-supplied address
- **What happens:** `verify_jwt=false`, no auth gate. A `test_mode` branch reads
  `{template_id, test_email}` and sends a rendered template to `test_email` —
  fully attacker-controlled, gated only by a valid template UUID. The normal
  (non-test) path additionally lets an anonymous caller force-flush the entire
  onboarding queue to real users on demand. A clean arbitrary-recipient send
  primitive.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/config.toml:49`;
  `supabase/functions/process-onboarding-emails/index.ts:69-124` (test branch),
  `:125+` (unauthenticated force-flush).
- **Blast radius:** Same as E-01 — arbitrary-recipient email-bomb and Resend
  reputation damage.
- **Fix size:** S

## P1 findings

### Money

> Note on severity: T1's adversarial verifiers kept all of these at P1 except
> where flagged. Two were **scope-narrowed** (not downgraded): M-05 is
> internally-consistent and not externally exploitable (trust/quote problem, not
> theft), and the double-collection follow-on of M-13's bulk-reset is blocked by

#### M-03 — First-payer discount silently not applied when the invoice payer isn't the first-selected player
- **What happens:** When the trainer picks someone other than the first-selected
  player to receive the invoice and adds a discount, the discount is recorded on
  the booking (`discount_amount = 10`) but never subtracted from what's charged.
  The customer is billed the full price while the books show a phantom discount.
- **Evidence:** `src/lib/bookForPlayerBooking.ts:69-75`;
  `src/lib/bookingPricing.ts:174-179` (`applyFirstPayerDiscount` returns unchanged
  unless `playerIndex===0`). Verified: payer = 2nd player, €50 / €10 discount →
  row charged €50.00 with `discount_amount=10`.
- **Blast radius:** Every booking using the payer selector with a discount.
  Customer overpays; `original − discount ≠ payment` corrupts any discount report.
- **Fix size:** S

#### M-04 — Adding a player from the calendar rebalances PAID and externally-paid bookings
- **What happens:** The calendar passes booked players *without* their payment
  status, so the "skip paid/external" filter sees `undefined` and rebalances
  **everyone** — including already-paid rows. A player who paid €50 has their
  booking silently rewritten to €25 and their discount zeroed. The paid invoice
  (€50) and the booking (€25) now disagree permanently.
- **Evidence:** `src/components/trainer/BookForPlayerDialog.tsx:674-676` (filter
  on absent fields); `src/pages/TrainerCalendar.tsx:177-185` and
  `src/pages/academy/AcademyCalendar.tsx` build players without payment fields;
  `rebalanceExistingOnSlot` at `:288-314`.
- **Blast radius:** Any split slot with already-paid participants when a player is
  added from TrainerCalendar or AcademyCalendar. (Verifier: OpenSlots is **not**
  affected — it passes no booked players.) Permanent silent corruption of paid
  money records.
- **Fix size:** S

#### M-05 — Dialog price summary disagrees with what's actually booked/invoiced
- **What happens:** The booking dialog's "Total" is computed from the hourly rate
  (ignoring a configured slot price), multiplied by player count (the real model
  charges the slot price once), and the percentage discount is applied to that
  inflated number as an absolute euro amount. The trainer quotes the customer the
  dialog total, but the invoice bills a completely different model — diverging in
  both directions.
- **Evidence:** `src/components/trainer/BookForPlayerDialog.tsx:357,361,364-368`
  vs the actual charging model in `src/lib/bookingPricing.ts:57-92`.
- **Blast radius:** Every >1-player or percentage-discount booking, and any slot
  whose configured price differs from the hourly-derived price. **Verifier scope:**
  not externally exploitable — charged/invoiced amounts follow the intended,
  internally-consistent model; the divergence is between the trainer's *quote* and
  reality. Kept P1 for billing-trust correctness.
- **Fix size:** M

#### M-06 — Duplicate-invoice guard misses superset batches; bookings invoiced twice as commitment batches grow
- **What happens:** The dedupe check only matches an existing invoice that is a
  *superset* of the requested bookings, and the DB unique index hashes the *exact*
  sorted set. So an invoice for `[A]` and a later one for `[A,B]` both succeed, and
  booking A is billed twice. The cycle-commitment cron re-derives each player's
  batch from all their claimed bookings on every run, and claims arrive across
  days — so batches grow naturally and double-bill on auto-sent invoices.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/functions/auto-create-invoice/index.ts:469-497` (`.contains`
  superset check); unique index on `md5(sorted booking_ids)` in
  `migrations/20260503101100`; batch growth in
  `generate-cycle-commitment-invoices/index.ts:69-115`. PGlite-reproduced.
  (Same finding as edge-track T5-2.5.)
- **Blast radius:** Every rebooked cycle where claims straddle multiple cron runs,
  plus finalize/add-player overlaps. Direct double-charge, auto-sent, no review.
- **Fix size:** M

#### M-07 — `finalize-proposals` splits by whole-cycle headcount instead of per-group; systematic undercharge on multi-group cycles
- **What happens:** When a proposals cycle has two independent groups of 4 on
  different slots, finalize counts all 8 players and bills each `price/8` instead
  of `price/4`. The trainer collects half. Invoices send immediately as "sent".
- **Evidence:** `supabase/functions/finalize-proposals/index.ts:206-222`
  (`totalUniquePlayers` over all slots), bookings created without `payment_amount`
  so auto-create uses `slot_price/N`. Contrast the correct per-group scoping in
  `_shared/cycle-commitment-invoicing.ts:96-105`.
- **Blast radius:** Every upfront split-payment proposals cycle with >1 group;
  50% loss at 2 groups, 67% at 3. Single-group cycles are billed correctly.
- **Fix size:** S

#### M-08 — `finalize-proposals` sweeps ALL pending bookings into new invoices, and concurrent finalize duplicates bookings
- **What happens:** After creating bookings, finalize re-queries *every*
  confirmed+pending booking on the cycle's slots and invoices the lot — including
  commitment bookings already invoiced and manually-added players. Combined with
  the superset dedup miss (M-06), the larger batch isn't blocked and re-bills. And
  two admins clicking "Finalize" at once both read `status='proposed'` before
  either updates (no transaction, no idempotency key, service-role bypasses the
  capacity trigger) → duplicate bookings + double invoices.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/functions/finalize-proposals/index.ts:88-169,185-241`;
  no unique index on `bookings(slot_id, player_id)`;
  `migrations/20260610220000_enforce_booking_slot_tier.sql:36-43` bypasses
  service-role. (Same finding as edge-track T5-2.2.)
- **Blast radius:** Any cycle mixing finalization with priority-claim commitments
  or manual adds, or two admins finalizing at once. Double-billing to "sent" plus
  capacity-consuming duplicate bookings.
- **Fix size:** M

#### M-09 — `recalculate-invoices` rebuild double-divides split shares and drops slot-level extra costs
- **What happens:** The admin maintenance function `rebuild_from_bookings`
  unconditionally divides each price by the detected split count — it's *missing*
  the "is this already a share?" guard that exists in the client code. One run with
  no `invoice_ids` rewrites **every** unpaid invoice, halving split totals and
  silently dropping any extra-cost line that lived on the slot rather than the
  cycle settings.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/functions/recalculate-invoices/index.ts:99-147` (no
  `paymentAmountsAreSplitShares` guard), `:156-178` (extra-cost fallback absent),
  `:233` (`changed` forced true so it always writes).
- **Blast radius:** Global on a single admin run. **Verifier nuance:** there is no
  UI or cron caller in the repo — corruption requires a deliberate
  `rebuild_from_bookings=true` invocation. It's a landmine on the documented
  maintenance path, not an automated/attacker-reachable flow. Default (no dry-run,
  no `invoice_ids`) is maximally destructive.
- **Fix size:** M

#### M-10 — Invoice numbering: academy numbers have zero uniqueness, and numbering is a non-atomic read-increment-write
- **What happens:** The only uniqueness constraint is `UNIQUE(trainer_id,
  invoice_number)`. Academy invoices have `trainer_id = NULL`, and Postgres treats
  NULLs as distinct, so academy invoices can **never** conflict — two admins (or
  two trainers under one academy) mint the same legal invoice number with
  different amounts. Numbering is read-increment-write in both the client and the
  edge function with the counter advanced *after* insert, so a lost update or a
  cross-player collision either duplicates a number or silently drops the invoice
  for a paid booking (the 23505 recovery only re-finds same-player invoices).
- **Evidence:** `migrations/20260118102744...sql:38` (only constraint);
  `src/components/invoices/CreateCustomInvoiceDialog.tsx:157-219`,
  `src/pages/academy/AcademyCreateInvoice.tsx:216-278` (client read-increment);
  `supabase/functions/auto-create-invoice/index.ts:499-528,578-606`. Merges T1
  CH-9 + CH-17 and UX-track T3-1.4.
- **Blast radius:** All academy manual invoices and all concurrent auto-invoicing
  (guaranteed bursts at cycle start). Duplicate/gapped legal numbering breaks NL
  Belastingdienst sequential-numbering requirements; missing invoices for paid
  lessons go undetected. The single-admin stale-counter variant doesn't even need
  concurrency.
- **Fix size:** M (needs a DB sequence / atomic increment + academy unique index)

#### M-11 — Editing or recalculating an invoice leaves a stale open Mollie payment that's reused without an amount check
- **What happens:** Editing a sent invoice, deleting a session, or a split-count
  rebalance rewrites the invoice total but never touches the live Mollie payment.
  The pay endpoint reuses any "open" payment with no amount comparison, so the
  customer pays the **old** amount. The webhook then refuses to mark it paid
  (amount mismatch) and returns 200 — money sits in the trainer's account, the
  invoice stays "sent" and overdue, and the player can be dunned again. Editing
  down makes the player overpay.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/functions/create-invoice-payment/index.ts:240-266`
  (reuses open payment, no amount check); edit paths
  `src/pages/academy/AcademyEditInvoice.tsx:215-233`,
  `src/pages/trainer/TrainerEditInvoice.tsx`,
  `src/components/invoices/EditInvoiceDialog.tsx:222-240`; recalc
  `src/lib/invoiceSync.ts:309-353`; webhook block
  `supabase/functions/mollie-webhook/index.ts:299-326`. Merges T1 CH-10 + CH-15 +
  CH-19.
- **Blast radius:** Any sent invoice edited or auto-recalculated while a pay link
  is alive — split rebalances, session removals and price edits are routine.
  Bounded by Mollie's open-payment window (minutes for iDEAL/cards, days for bank
  transfer). Detection is ops-only (Slack + 30-min health check); no automated
  reconciliation.
- **Fix size:** M

#### M-12 — Cancelled invoices can still be paid: cancel never voids the Mollie payment, and the webhook has no status guard
- **What happens:** Cancelling an invoice only sets `status='cancelled'` — it never
  calls Mollie to cancel the open checkout. The webhook updates by id with no check
  on the prior status, so when a customer later completes the old checkout, the
  cancelled invoice flips to "paid" and its bookings flip to confirmed/paid. If a
  corrected invoice was already issued and paid, you've collected twice.
- **Evidence:** cancel paths
  `src/pages/trainer/TrainerInvoices.tsx:335`,
  `src/pages/academy/AcademyEditInvoice.tsx:291` (no Mollie cancel anywhere — grep
  of `api.mollie.com` finds only create/fetch/oauth); webhook
  `supabase/functions/mollie-webhook/index.ts:328-331` (unconditional update).
  Merges T1 CH-11 + CH-18.
- **Blast radius:** Every cancel-and-reissue workflow. **Verifier scope:** the
  public-token-revoke trigger (migration 20260511174838) closes the
  re-click-the-email-link path; the live window is "Mollie checkout still open /
  in-flight at cancel time" — notably bank-transfer methods that stay open for
  days. Within that window the resurrection is fully unguarded and silent.
- **Fix size:** S (add a `status != 'cancelled'` guard + cancel the Mollie payment)

#### M-13 — No terminal invoice states: paid invoices can be edited, cancelled, or bulk-reset to draft (clearing `paid_at`)
- **What happens:** "Save" on an edit page and "Reset to draft" in bulk have no
  status gate. A trainer cleaning up can select paid invoices (the select-all
  includes them) and reset them to draft with `paid_at` erased — the record that
  money was received survives only at Mollie. Re-sending then renumbers and emails
  a dead pay link. Editing a paid invoice's amounts makes the ledger and the
  archived PDF disagree.
- **Evidence:** edit save with no gate
  `src/pages/trainer/TrainerEditInvoice.tsx:381`,
  `src/pages/academy/AcademyEditInvoice.tsx:202-256`; bulk reset
  `src/pages/trainer/TrainerInvoices.tsx:307-323`,
  `src/pages/academy/AcademyInvoices.tsx:498-503`. Merges T1 CH-12 + CH-20 and
  UX-track T3-4.6.
- **Blast radius:** Whole-ledger integrity — settled revenue can vanish from
  "paid" totals; exports diverge from history. **Verifier scope on the bulk-reset
  variant (CH-20):** the double-collection follow-on is blocked by the
  public-token-revoke trigger; remaining harm is irreversible one-click loss of
  `paid_at`/audit state, paid/booking divergence, inflated unpaid totals and
  confusing dunning emails — kept high as a data-integrity issue.
- **Fix size:** M

#### M-14 — Stale Mollie webhook (expired/failed) cancels bookings that were already paid out-of-band
- **What happens:** The webhook applies `failed`/`expired`/`canceled` statuses
  unconditionally to the booking rows. If a player starts an online payment, then
  pays the trainer in cash (recorded via mark-paid, which doesn't clear the Mollie
  id), the later expiry webhook flips those paid bookings to cancelled/failed —
  freeing the slot for double-booking while the trainer already has the money.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/functions/mollie-webhook/index.ts:443-462,499-517` (no
  guard on current booking state); manual mark-paid leaves `mollie_payment_id` set
  at `src/lib/unpaidBookings.ts:239-249`, `src/pages/TrainerEarnings.tsx:274-282`.
  Merges T1 CH-14 + lower LO-32.
- **Blast radius:** Any booking with a once-created Mollie payment plus a later
  out-of-band state change. Silently un-confirms paid lessons, corrupts earnings,
  enables double-booking.
- **Fix size:** S

#### M-15 — Duplicate payment creation orphans the paid Mollie payment; webhook becomes unroutable and a second checkout is issued
- **What happens:** `create-mollie-payment` has no open-payment reuse check, and
  `create-invoice-payment`'s reuse check is read-then-act. Two clicks (or a
  paid-but-not-yet-webhooked race) create two payments; the DB keeps only the
  newest id and may clear the id for a payment that's actually paid. The webhook
  resolves the connected-account token *only* by matching that id, so it can't
  route the first payment — refuses with 200. Real money taken, booking/invoice
  stays unpaid, and a second payable checkout exists (double-charge vector).
- **Evidence:** `create-mollie-payment/index.ts:457-488,604-607`;
  `create-invoice-payment/index.ts:240-266,385-391`; webhook resolution
  `mollie-webhook/index.ts:184-205,240-249`. Merges T1 CH-16 and edge-track
  T5-2.1.
- **Blast radius:** The public invoice/booking pay flow (primary collection path).
  One stuck-unpaid paid invoice per occurrence plus a live double-payment vector;
  only a Slack "refused" alert and a daily cron hint at it.
- **Fix size:** M

#### M-16 — Cyclus delete recalculates only invoices overlapping the opened slot; other invoices keep billing for cascade-deleted sessions
- **What happens:** "Delete entire cyclus" cancels bookings across all cycle slots,
  but only recalculates invoices that overlap the *single* slot the dialog was
  opened from. A player who joined mid-cycle (invoice covers slots 2–10) is
  untouched when you delete from slot 1. The bookings then cascade-delete, leaving
  the invoice "sent" and fully payable via its public link for sessions that no
  longer exist — and the data needed to fix it is destroyed.

#### E-03 — Type confusion on name fields throws a 500 in the public `submit-guest-intake` endpoint
- **What happens:** A non-string `firstName`/`lastName` reaches `.trim()` with no

#### E-04 — Public intake: unbounded payloads and a spoofable IP rate-limit enable storage bloat and email-spray to arbitrary addresses
- **What happens:** `submit-guest-intake` inserts `metadata`/`notes`/arrays with no

  attacker-controlled `X-Forwarded-For` header. Rotating that header resets the

  send "registration confirmation" emails to arbitrary victim addresses well beyond
  the cap.
- **Evidence:** `submit-guest-intake/index.ts:77,237-246`; confirmation email to
  request-supplied address (`~:320`). Merges T5-0.2 + T5-3.3.
- **Blast radius:** Storage/cost abuse and arbitrary-recipient email-spray on a
  public endpoint.
- **Fix size:** M

#### E-05 — `send-email` rejects all admin intake notifications, so trainers are never notified of new registrations
- **What happens:** The `new_intake_registration_admin` type is treated as a
  "public form" and forced to `to === "info@padeltrainer.ai"`, but

  calls returns 400 — admins never learn about new cycle registrations.
- **Evidence:** `supabase/functions/send-email/index.ts:1151,1190`;
  `submit-guest-intake/index.ts:483-508`. (This is the "broken admin-notification

- **Fix size:** S

#### E-06 — Public unauthenticated endpoints leak raw DB `error.message` (schema/SQL/constraint internals)
- **What happens:** Public endpoints return raw Postgres error text verbatim,

- **Evidence:** `submit-guest-intake/index.ts:189,255`; `public-api/index.ts:97`;

- **What happens:** Email addresses are interpolated into edge-function logs in
  user-facing flows — exactly the PII the recent telemetry-hardening commit aimed
  to remove. Anyone with log access (support, a leaked export, an over-broad log
  integration) can harvest emails tied to signups, deletions and intakes.
- **Evidence:** `signup-user/index.ts:334,337`;
  `submit-guest-intake/index.ts:425`; `request-account-deletion/index.ts:82`;

  `send-digest-emails`, `send-schedule-notifications`.
- **Blast radius:** Privacy posture regression across many flows.
- **Fix size:** M

#### E-08 — `send-campaign-emails` has no atomic per-recipient claim or status gate; a concurrent/retried request double-sends the whole campaign
- **What happens:** It fetches all `pending` recipients and marks each "sent" only
  after Resend succeeds, with no campaign-status gate and no atomic claim (unlike
  the onboarding queue, which got the phase-5 claim RPC). A 150-recipient campaign
  takes 30+ seconds, so a client timeout + re-click, or two managers clicking,
  sends every recipient the campaign twice.
- **Evidence:** `send-campaign-emails/index.ts:118-158,164-218`; the unused
  template `claim_onboarding_email_queue_item` in
  `migrations/20260606120000_phase5_email_idempotency_and_cron_ficwb.sql:10-35`.
- **Blast radius:** Every multi-recipient campaign.
- **Fix size:** M

#### E-09 — `send-digest-emails` and `process-onboarding-emails` normal paths allow unauthenticated queue force-flush
- **What happens:** Both have `verify_jwt=false` and no auth check on the normal
  path. Any anonymous caller can trigger an immediate flush, defeating
  daily/weekly batching (users get digests at attacker-chosen times) and risking

- **Evidence:** `supabase/config.toml:130,49`;
  `send-digest-emails/index.ts:127+,146-153`.
- **Blast radius:** Premature/duplicate digest and onboarding sends.
- **Fix size:** S

## What's healthy

Credit where it's due — several areas came back clean under scrutiny.

- **Production data integrity is genuinely clean today.** The T2 X-ray checked 23
  invariants against the live database; **21 are at zero**: no orphaned bookings,
  **no duplicate invoice numbers to date**, no double bookings, no money-state
  contradictions, no dangling invoice→booking links, no temporal nonsense. The two
  non-zero results are both benign: the 159 amount-less academy `line_items` (D-01,
  display-correct) and 110 bookings carrying both `player_id` and
  `guest_player_id` — **all 110 match the designed account-claim flow by design**
  (player_id = the guest's linked profile id), zero anomalies. _Important nuance:_
  T1 found invoice-numbering **races at the code level** (M-10, M-17) and T2
  confirms **no duplicates have actually happened yet** — both statements are true.
  The races are latent, not realized; that's exactly why fixing them before more
  concurrency (more academies, more cycle-start payment bursts) is the right call.
- **Double-submit protection is broadly solid.** All seven named heavyweight forms
  and ~20 other dialogs/flows were verified protected (BookForPlayerDialog,
  InlineBookPlayer, CycleApplicationForm, MergePlayersDialog, BulkCopySlotsWizard,
  BookLesson, PublicInvoicePay, the onboarding flows, etc.). The gaps (U-05, U-18,
  U-19, U-20) are the exceptions, not the rule.
- **Several retry/idempotency paths are correct by construction.** The phase-5
  atomic email-claim works for the onboarding queue; `mollie-webhook` amount
  verification blocks underpayment and handles *sequential* duplicate deliveries
  cleanly; `verify-mollie-payment` and `stripe-subscription-webhook` are idempotent.
- **RLS / auth posture** from the earlier security audits held up — the issues
  found here are app-logic and abuse-surface, not broken row-level security.
- **Performance fundamentals are strong.** Marketing assets, route-splitting, table
  rendering and offset pagination are all clean; the perf findings are bundle
  quick-wins (P-04, P-05) and one quadratic RPC that only matters at 10k+ players.
